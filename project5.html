Mobile Grocery List App

The UX/UI team at AdAdapted imagined a user-friendly grocery list mobile app that combined all of the best parts of list apps and the company’s core advertising technology.

Overview

Problem

Part of AdAdapted’s core business relies on integrating its patented "add-to-list" advertising into partner apps. To expand our offerings and deepen our understanding of users’ behavior, we needed to design our own grocery list app that could serve as a model for a best-in-class user experience.

Solution

Design a user-friendly grocery list-building mobile app to host AdAdapted's add-to-list technology while including innovative features to promote user loyalty and customizability.

Users

A future network of consumers who use this app for their everyday grocery planning and shopping.

Impact

While the app was a conceptual exploration and not fully implemented, it served as a successful proof of concept and sparked further opportunities for innovation. The project's findings and prototypes are being used to inform future mobile development and provide a comprehensive understanding of the competitive landscape.

Research

I conducted extensive user research to understand how and why people use mobile grocery list apps. The research included personas, scenarios, and comparative analyses of ten competing apps. I then conducted in-depth interviews with six employees who are avid grocery app users.

Key Interview Questions:

  1. How do you typically organize your grocery list? What does your typical grocery trip look like?
  2. What influences your grocery shopping decisions? (Recipes, restocking the pantry, etc.)
  3. Think of an app that you use all the time - why do you like the app? What motivates you to keep using it?
  4. What apps do you use for grocery planning, and what features do you find most valuable? When do you find the app most useful?
  5. What features are missing from current apps that would improve your experience?

Our findings revealed that a successful app must:

  1. Streamline In-Store Shopping: Offer quick list creation and an intuitive way to check off items, as the in-store experience is crucial for user satisfaction.
  2. Integrate Seamlessly: Design the app to be convenient and fit naturally into a shopper's routine, ideally by replicating the physical in-store shopping experience and including information on prices, deals, and inventory.
  3. Simplify Decision-Making: Include features that are simple yet fun, making it easier for users to make smart decisions during both list planning and in-store shopping.
  4. Encourage Customization: Provide options for users to organize their lists by store, aisle, or personal preference.
  5. Boost Loyalty: Add features like recipe inspiration and easy list sharing to provide extra value and encourage long-term use.

For a more comprehensive review of our research and findings, please see the full report: List App Research and Findings (PDF)

Design Process

The design process was highly iterative, culminating in a full high-fidelity prototype of a mobile app MVP for both iOS and Android.

I used Figma to build reusable components, and I designed and prototyped complex animations in After Effects and LottieFiles to ensure a high-quality visual experience. Project deliverables also included detailed specs for accessible micro-interactions, gestures, and haptic feedback.

The final app design included quick list-building features, streamlined onboarding, multiple list organization options, product suggestions, and an optimized in-store view to improve usability. We expanded our scope by designing an additional desktop experience for list-building on the web.
